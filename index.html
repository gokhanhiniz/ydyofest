The Ancient Olympic Games:
More Than Sport

Nearly three millennia ago, an athletic tradition began that was capable of halting wars. What did these games truly mean to the ancient Greeks?

By Nehir Gümüş Advisor: Didem Sevgi Avcı  ·  B203
Original illustration — The sanctuary of Olympia, where the ancient games honoured Zeus with sport, sacrifice, and the Sacred Truce. © YDYO Fest 2026

Today we see the Olympics as a global sports event, but they actually started nearly 2,800 years ago as a sacred tradition with the power to stop wars. These games were held in Olympia to honour the god Zeus. They brought different Greek cities together and created a time of peace through sport. The Ancient Olympic Games had three main purposes: to honour Zeus, to promote peace between city-states, and to showcase the physical excellence of the athletes.

"They transformed a simple competition into a powerful tool for unity and worship."

Nehir Gümüş

First of all, the games were a religious festival where athletes offered sacrifices to Zeus. Since the Greeks believed their physical strength was a gift from the gods, they competed to show their gratitude. For this reason, religious ceremonies were just as important as the races themselves. In fact, the central event was not a sport but a massive sacrifice of one hundred oxen at the altar of Zeus to demonstrate their devotion.

In addition, the Olympics played a vital political role by creating a Sacred Truce. This meant that all wars between Greek city-states had to stop so that everyone could travel to and attend the event safely. This tradition helped different Greek people feel like they belonged to one shared culture, even if they lived in rival cities. During this period, the common identity of being Greek became more important than the local interests of individual cities.

Finally, the event allowed individuals to show their strength and gain great honour by winning an olive wreath. Winners did not receive money; instead, they gained eternal fame and respect in their society. Returning home as heroes, a winner's name would often be remembered for many generations. Statues were even built in their honour to inspire young people to train hard, live virtuously, and serve their community.

In conclusion, the Ancient Olympics were much more than a sport. They were a unique combination of religion, politics, and personal achievement that shaped Greek culture. They transformed a simple competition into a powerful tool for unity and worship.

The Psychological Dynamics of Retail Therapy

Why do people reach for their wallets after a difficult day? The answer lies deeper than a simple shopping habit.

By Irmak İlhan Advisor: Didem Sevgi Avcı  ·  B203
Original illustration — The colourful allure of retail: shopping temporarily boosts dopamine and offers a sense of control in an uncertain world. © YDYO Fest 2026

Have you ever wondered why people head to shopping malls or browse online stores after a stressful day? While there is no specific time for shopping, the need for it often arises after an intense and stressful day. This condition is commonly defined as retail therapy. Retail therapy is not only a superficial habit; it is a complex psychological behaviour driven by the need for emotional regulation, the desire to regain a sense of control, and the persuasive influence of modern marketing.

"Retail therapy is much more than just spending money. It is a way for people to manage their emotions and respond to various stimuli."

Irmak İlhan

First of all, one of the most significant reasons for retail therapy is emotional regulation. When individuals feel anxious or sad, they often experience a drop in their dopamine levels. Shopping provides happiness and excitement, which triggers a dopamine release. For many, finding the perfect item serves as an essential tool to reduce anxiety and lift their mood instantly.

Secondly, shopping helps people restore their sense of control. In daily life, individuals may encounter situations that make them feel powerless. Being the decision-maker during a shopping experience provides a feeling of empowerment. Research indicates that these small choices reduce anxiety levels by allowing individuals to gain a sense of authority over their environment.

Finally, marketing tactics play a major role in this psychological behaviour. Stores are designed with pleasant music, specific scents, and attractive displays to influence consumers. Furthermore, social media platforms create a sense of necessity through special offers such as limited-time discounts. Consequently, producers understand these psychological triggers and establish an emotional bond with the consumer in this manner.

In conclusion, retail therapy is much more than just spending money. It is a way for people to manage their emotions, provide a sense of control, and respond to various stimuli. Although retail therapy appears to provide happiness and excitement, it is essentially one of the primary ways of coping with problems in social life.

Staying Mysterious on Social Media

In an age of relentless oversharing, the case for keeping something back from the digital gaze.

By Dilara İşleyen Advisor: Didem Sevgi Avcı  ·  B203
Original illustration — The digital gaze: every share places us under perpetual spectatorship, slowly eroding the boundaries of private life. © YDYO Fest 2026

"Do not show the extent of your depth: it is the greatest rule in the art of living," says Baltasar Gracián. These days, with the rise of the digital age, almost everyone of all ages has a social media account. As a result, many people share a great deal about their lives in cyberspace. Excessive sharing on social networks can negatively impact people's lives due to reasons such as addiction and violation of privacy, so there are significant motivations to reduce excessive sharing about personal life. Therefore, reducing oversharing on social media is essential for preserving privacy boundaries, avoiding dependence on external validation, and preventing the superficialization of social relationships.

"Everything that was once considered private now becomes a social commodity, and privacy is effectively eroded."

Dilara İşleyen

One of the primary reasons for reducing social media sharing is the erosion of privacy boundaries. By sharing on social media, individuals constantly place themselves under public spectatorship, which pushes them to produce ever more material for that spectatorship and further expose their privacy. Everything that was once considered private, such as relationships, daily life, and emotions, now becomes a social commodity, and privacy is effectively eroded.

Beyond this, reducing social media sharing is related to dependence on external validation. Increased dependence on external approval creates a cycle. This cycle is explained in psychology by the concept of operant conditioning. In particular, an unpredictable variable reward system increases the likelihood that a behaviour will be repeated. In this way, individuals come to modify their self-worth through external feedback.

In addition, sharing too much on social platforms can lead to superficiality in relationships. Revealing too much changes the structure of relationships. Communications tend to be more about being watched and getting a reaction than about building genuine connections. As sharing increases, messages and calls become more superficial, and deep connections are not formed. People focus more on how someone appears rather than who they truly are.

In conclusion, reducing oversharing on social media is necessary for wholesome relationships and healthier psychology. A more selective and intentional approach to sharing not only preserves psychological autonomy but also strengthens the quality of social relationships.

History or Hollywood?
Debunking Three Legendary Myths

From flat-earth voyagers to slave-built pyramids, the stories we are taught to believe deserve a second look.

By Eray Aslan Advisor: Ahu Akbay  ·  B202
Original illustration — Three legendary myths reimagined: Columbus and the spherical Earth, the gladiator's professional arena, and the skilled pyramid builders. © YDYO Fest 2026

Have you ever worried that the facts you believe to be true might actually be false? History may not always be a compilation of absolute facts. Sometimes, it may represent elements of a deliberately constructed and systematically executed scheme. We usually believe popular historical stories that we hear from our elders or read in books without questioning them. However, when examined more closely, many famous historical "facts" are actually myths. The following three examples demonstrate that many widely accepted historical narratives are, in fact, misconceptions.

"The real stories behind these events are often far more fascinating than the legends we are taught to believe."

Eray Aslan

One of the most common historical misconceptions is the belief that Christopher Columbus set out to prove that the Earth was round. In reality, educated people had known that the Earth was spherical since the time of Ancient Greek scholars such as Pythagoras and Aristotle. Columbus's disagreement with many scholars was not about the shape of the Earth, but rather about the size of the oceans and the distance required to reach Asia by sailing westward. The flat-Earth myth was popularised by the American writer Washington Irving in 1828.

Another major misconception concerns Roman gladiators. Thanks to Hollywood films, we often imagine gladiators as slaves who fought to the death in every match. However, the reality was much closer to modern-day professional sports. Gladiators were extremely valuable to their owners. It cost a great deal of money to train them, house them, and provide them with proper healthcare. Because of these high costs, killing a defeated gladiator was quite rare. Surprisingly, successful gladiators became celebrities of their time and earned large amounts of money.

Finally, the construction of the Great Pyramids is also frequently misunderstood. Modern archaeological discoveries have completely debunked the slave-builder myth. In the 1990s, archaeologists Mark Lehner and Zahi Hawass discovered a massive workers' village near the Giza Pyramid Complex. They found evidence suggesting that the construction workers were respectable citizens, skilled craftsmen who were fed a high-protein diet and received advanced medical care. They were even buried in special graves near the pharaohs, a sacred honour that would never have been granted to slaves.

In summary, history is rarely as simple as it appears on the surface. Revealing these truths reminds us that we should approach historical accounts with a critical mind. The real stories behind these events are often far more fascinating than the legends we are taught to believe.

The Power of Early Rising

The early bird catches the worm, the proverb tells us. Science and habit suggest the wisdom in that old saying runs surprisingly deep.

By Havin Uysal Advisor: Merve Nihal Tan  ·  BP107
Original illustration — The quiet magic of dawn: an early riser greets the sunrise as birds stir and the world is still. © YDYO Fest 2026

Have you ever wondered why some people feel remarkably more energetic when they wake up early in the morning? Many people believe that waking up early helps them have a more productive and active day overall. As the proverb goes, the early bird catches the worm, which means people who start their day early often have a significant advantage over others. While some individuals naturally prefer staying up late, others choose to wake up early and follow a disciplined daily routine to reach their goals. In my opinion, being an early riser is better for one's energy because it improves focus and helps people develop a regular and healthy routine.

"Early mornings are usually quiet, so there are fewer distractions. People can use their energy in a more effective way and finish their work faster."

Havin Uysal

First of all, waking up early can substantially improve concentration and mental clarity. In the morning, the mind is usually fresh because the body has recently undergone a full period of rest. This makes it easier to think clearly and comprehend new information more efficiently throughout the day. For example, students who wake up early often find it easier to study, complete their assignments, and stay focused on their lessons. In addition, early mornings are usually quiet, so there are fewer distractions such as loud noise or social media notifications. Due to this peaceful environment, people can use their energy in a more effective way and finish their work faster. As a result, they may feel more satisfied and less stressed while performing their daily tasks.

Secondly, being an early riser helps people build a regular and balanced routine. When individuals go to bed and wake up at the same time every day, their bodies gradually get used to this schedule. This habit helps regulate their internal biological clock and improves overall sleep quality. As a result, they feel less tired and more energetic throughout the entire day. Moreover, having a regular routine makes it easier to plan daily activities such as studying, exercising, or relaxing with friends. For instance, early risers often have enough time to eat a healthy breakfast, prepare mentally for the day, and even engage in physical exercise like jogging or yoga. These healthy habits can significantly increase both physical and mental stamina in the long term.

In conclusion, starting the day early is more advantageous for one's energy levels as it enhances mental concentration and fosters a consistent, healthy lifestyle. Waking up early can help people feel more energetic and productive, as long as they get enough sleep and follow a balanced lifestyle to maintain their well-being.

About the Festival

Language Meets Creativity

YDYO FEST 2026 is the annual Language, Culture & Creativity Festival organized by the School of Foreign Languages at Çanakkale Onsekiz Mart University. Students and faculty come together for oral presentations, poster exhibitions, drama, music concerts, trivia, a piano recital — all in English.

From ancient Egyptian mythology to colour psychology, from Macedonian folk songs to a mini concert — YDYO FEST celebrates language as a bridge between cultures.
